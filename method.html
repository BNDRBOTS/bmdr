The method

Left. Right. Repeat.

Bilateral stimulation is rhythmic left–right sensory input — the core mechanic behind EMDR-style practice. BMDR delivers it through two channels you can use together or separately: your eyes and your ears.

The visual channel

A soft, glowing anchor travels across the screen and your eyes travel with it. You choose how it moves — a straight Gentle Sway or a continuous figure-eight Infinite Flow — and how fast, from a near-still 0.2 Hz to a brisk 3.0 Hz. The screen holds nothing else: no menus, no numbers, no noise.

The audio channel

A low 87 Hz base tone — layered one octave below and one octave above — pans gently between your left and right ear. Keep it locked to the visual rhythm with Link Rhythms, or give each channel its own pace. It is off until you turn it on.

A session, start to finish

Five moments.
No instructions needed.

01 — Stillness

The space opens quiet. A status light reads Stillness. Nothing moves until you decide it should.

Idle
02 — Begin

Tap anywhere — the whole screen is the play button. The anchor starts its sweep at your saved pace.

One tap
03 — Follow

Eyes track the anchor. If audio is on, the tone crosses left to right in rhythm. That's the entire job.

In motion
04 — Adjust

Too fast, too small, wrong color? Slide the bottom sheet up and tune pace, size, pattern, palette, and sound without ending the session.

Mid-session
05 — Return

Tap again to rest, or step away — the session pauses itself the moment you leave the tab or lock your phone.

Why self-guided matters

A practice you control is a practice you keep. There's no appointment to book, no commute, no waiting room — the space is open at 3 PM and at 3 AM. You set the pace literally, with a slider, and change it the moment your body asks for something different.

What BMDR refuses to be

No streaks. No badges. No push notifications asking where you've been. Wellness apps love to gamify calm until it becomes another obligation — BMDR deliberately doesn't. It's a room, not a regimen. It's there when you arrive and silent when you leave.

Said plainly

BMDR is a self-guided wellness tool for personal practice. It is not EMDR therapy, it is not medical treatment, and it is not a substitute for working with a licensed therapist. It gives you the bilateral mechanic — clean, adjustable, and private — and leaves the clinical work to clinicians. If you're in crisis, contact local emergency services.
